# Skill: draft-brief

Build one enabling-gap brief from the confirmed evidence, into
`templates/enabling-gap-brief.html`. One page per gap; clients name these among the
most valuable artefacts, and the value is the discipline of the content model.

## Inputs

The gap's row in the confirmed landscape (enabler, status, waiting count, wave,
owner); the observations evidencing it; the opportunities waiting on it; the facts
manifest.

## Output

The populated brief: what the gap is, the skills it needs, what is missing and why,
what it releases, and the routes.

## Rules that bite here

- **Routes are options, not a recommendation, and each states what it assumes**,
  because the assumption is usually the thing to test first. Options are often buy,
  configure or decide; a missing owner is itself a finding. "Nothing assumed, this is
  enforcement of an existing rule" is a legitimate and strong entry.
- **No day estimates.** Skills are derivable from the work; days are not, without
  scope and a named doer, and a number against work nobody has been asked to do
  implies a decision that has not been made.
- **Skill ratings have three answers**: have it, absent, and have it committed
  elsewhere. Committed elsewhere is a scheduling decision, not a capability gap, and
  is usually the dominant answer; the capacity framing paragraph argues it from the
  discovery evidence.
- Anchor "what is missing" in the concrete: what the existing rules or systems
  already require, the incident or observation that makes it real (de-identified),
  and the voice of the person closest to it, paraphrased, who usually names the risk
  better than any policy clause.
- The releases list carries impact chips per opportunity, from the landscape, never
  re-graded here.
- Every figure from the facts manifest; roles never names; the footer carries the
  tokenisation note.

## Gate

The consultant reads each brief before it enters the pack, checking the routes'
assumptions are honest and the anchor incident is de-identified beyond recovery.
