# Judgement rubric: the report

The deterministic half is `check.py`. This is the half regex cannot do.

Run this against the draft in a session that has **not** written it. A model marking its
own work grades generously, so use a fresh context, and give it only the draft, this
rubric, and the house register exemplar.

## How to grade

For each criterion return **pass**, **weak** or **fail**, with a quoted line from the
draft as evidence. A verdict without a quotation is not a verdict.

**Default to fail when uncertain.** The cost of a false pass is a bad report reaching an
executive who reads closely. The cost of a false fail is one more look.

Do not soften. Do not summarise the report back. Grade it.

---

## 1. Does each section lead with its conclusion?

The house pattern is the conclusion first, then the evidence beneath it. The common
failure is a section that opens by describing what it contains, or by restating the
question.

**Fail** if a section opens with "This section sets out..." or similar, or if the reader
has to reach the third paragraph to learn what the section concluded.

## 2. Does every finding carry all three parts?

Declarative, then implication, then recommendation. What exists, what it means, what to
do. The recognisable failure mode is a draft that stops at declarative over and over.

**Fail** if findings state a fact and move on. Name the ones that stop short.

## 3. Is any section exclusively negative, and does the whole land near 60/40?

An assessment whose areas are mostly gaps drifts negative without active effort. List
the genuine strengths in the evidence before grading this criterion, then check they
appear in the draft.

**Fail** if any section is unrelieved criticism, or if the report reads as a catalogue
of what the client has not done.

## 4. Does anything read as a pitch for a second phase?

The explicit trap. The report must stand alone and stay useful months later, whether or
not the author is in the room.

**Fail** on any "we would", "the next phase", "our recommended engagement", or any
passage whose logic only works if the authors are hired again.

## 5. Are the worked examples framed as the framework applied?

One or two concrete picks are wanted, but as demonstrations of the method, not as the
report's conclusion. If circumstances change the picks change; the framework should not.

**Fail** if a pick reads as the answer, or if removing it would collapse the report.

## 6. Does the framework actually work without the author?

The test is whether a reader could take it, apply it to their own situation in six
months under a different org structure, and reach "these are the things we start with"
with confidence rather than a finger in the air.

**Fail** if the framework is described rather than operable, if it needs judgement only
the author has, or if it cannot be re-run against changed circumstances.

## 7. Is "where the value concentrates" built from the data?

It must trace to things that exist: what each gap gates, opportunity mass, cross-team
span, source counts, how much is already underway.

**Fail** on any composite index, weighted score or invented multiplier. A number that
looks rigorous and cannot be traced is worse than no number, and a close reader will
pull it apart.

## 8. Is the sponsor's hardest question answered honestly?

Every engagement acquires one: a question the sponsor asked directly that the evidence
only partly supports (a cost question against a productivity scope is the recurring
example). The evidence can speak to what it measured; it cannot support conclusions from
a scope it was not gathered under.

**Fail** if the report implies conclusions the evidence does not carry. **Also fail** if
it dodges the question entirely, because it was asked directly and silence is its own
answer.

## 9. Are absence and non-examination kept apart?

Some coverage is always deliberately shallow, usually at the client's request.

**Fail** on any sentence saying something is not there when the truth is it was not
examined.

## 10. Register

Compare against the house exemplar supplied with this rubric. What good looks like:
verdict first; concrete figures carried inline; one bolded sentence doing the work; the
risk named plainly, without hedging or apology.

**Fail** if the draft hedges where the evidence is solid, pads with parallel
constructions, or reads as though it is being careful rather than being clear.

## 11. Quotations

Target is zero. Quotes read as lazy and out of context; source material is paraphrased
into the narrative.

**Fail** on any quoted interview material. The deterministic checker only catches
quotations over about forty-five characters, so short ones are yours to find.

---

## Output

A table of the eleven criteria with verdicts, then the three most damaging problems in
priority order, each with the line to change and what to change it to.

Finish with one line: **would you put your name on this in front of a CEO who reads
closely?** Yes or no, and if no, the single reason.
