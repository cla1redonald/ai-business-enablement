# The engagement arc

The kit runs in two shapes. One is the six-week reference arc, which has been proven end
to end. The other is the two-week fast diagnostic, which is the kit's main scenario. Its
day-by-day runbook is not written yet. It will go in `fast-path-runbook.md` once the
fast path has been rehearsed end to end, which is the kit's next planned build step.

Both shapes deliver the same core. Conversations and documents come in, and they produce
observations tied to evidence, opportunities placed on value streams, and the 20-area
readiness assessment rolled up into five enablers. The output is a report whose In brief
can stand on its own, a versioned pack, and a choosing session in the diary.

## The six-week reference arc

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Kickoff, chasing access, every interview invite sent, the first 3 to 4 discovery sessions, drop-in 1 (the taster plus the topic menu poll) |
| 2 to 3 | Bulk interviews, departmental sessions, drop-in 2, first quick-win pairing |
| 3 to 4 | Mop-up interviews, readiness examination (console reviews, policy reads), drop-in 3, sync with any in-flight client initiative that overlaps the discovery |
| 5 | Synthesis: observations, opportunities, streams, the readiness ratings, the bridge |
| 6 | Report drafting, checking and review cycle; pack build; delivery and the choosing-session offer |

## Pacing rules (learned the expensive way)

- Getting interviews done is what sets the pace of the whole project. Nothing else can
  start until sessions are in the diary, holidays eat whole weeks, and there may be a
  dozen leads to book. Send every invite in week one, or the schedule never recovers.
- Track the days against the budget as you go. On a fixed-scope engagement, log the days
  used after every working session. Days you do not use cannot be billed later, and any
  overrun comes out of margin.
- Ring-fence a full day for the readout. The readout is what sells the next piece of
  work, so it must not be squeezed into whatever time is left.
- A wary client decides whether the work is landing well before the readout, usually
  within the first fortnight. Put visible value early, through the first drop-in, a
  first quick win and a first playback.
- Deliver quick wins during discovery. Pairing with someone on real work does more for
  acceptance than any argument in the report, and it gives the report its own evidence
  of momentum.
- Make sure the sponsor sees the room engage at least twice before the readout. Visible
  demand from beyond the sponsor is the strongest asset an engagement has, and the
  drop-ins that create it are the first thing to get squeezed.
- Ask what the client needs to protect. A stretched team is a constraint to design
  around rather than an obstacle, and a quick win reshaped to cost a protected team
  almost nothing is a better quick win.
- Debrief within the hour. Write up each call before the next one starts. A recollection
  written down straight away and marked as a recollection can be used as evidence.
  Anything written later is closer to fiction, however confident it feels.
- Plan recording for the meetings that matter. Before an important call, decide which
  one recorder will be running. If recording fails, ask the client for their own
  summary, which is better evidence than your recollection anyway.
- Run an early playback, at the end of week two, because it builds trust. Group the
  interview themes by the effort needed to unblock them (self-serve, assisted, needs
  investment), and never by the consultant's own skillset. Keep value and feasibility on
  separate axes, and present quick wins as proof that the approach works rather than as
  the prize.

## The two-week fast diagnostic (the outline, ahead of the runbook)

| Days | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 | Intake: kickoff, doc ask, all interviews booked, first sessions run, summaries same-day |
| 3 to 5 | Remaining interviews; observations and candidate opportunities as they land |
| 6 to 8 | Readiness interviews and direct examination (console, policies, connector list) |
| 8 to 9 | Landscape: streams, grades, gates, the bridge |
| 9 to 10 | Report from the skeleton, checks, pack, choosing session booked |

The fast path cuts three things from the six-week version. Step-level maps are drawn
only for the top 3 to 5 streams, drop-ins become optional (one taster at most), and
briefs are written only for gaps that gate the top opportunities. Some things it never
cuts. It still uses real transcripts rather than summaries, runs the whole readiness
instrument, and uses the facts manifest, the checker and the versioned pack, with the In
brief written first.

## The deliverable set

The client receives one pack (see `templates/pack-readme.md`). It holds the report,
workbook, maps, briefs, call summaries, a README and a CLAUDE.md written for the
client's AI, and it is baselined so it cannot be overwritten, then zipped. The choosing
session is offered for the week after the pack goes out. It is facilitated, and it runs
on the client's own workbook.
