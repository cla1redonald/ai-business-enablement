# 1. In brief

Caldergate Freight commissioned this work against a commercial moment. The growth plan
is to win larger retail importers, the pitch to them is speed and visibility, and the
sponsor's own verdict is that the back office is currently neither. By agreement the
work assessed the enabling layer, meaning how Caldergate's 140 people and its
processes use AI, and what has to be true before that use can scale.

The discovery recorded 21 opportunities across 6 value streams, resting on 47
observations drawn from 9 sources. Appetite is not what limits Caldergate. An interim
usage note is in force with a review-before-reliance clause most firms this size lack.
Real tools have been built unprompted, a booking-entry assistant on the operations
desk and assistant-generated margin views in finance, and the sales desk already
drafts with the assistant daily. What stands between Caldergate and most of the value
is groundwork. The 21 opportunities share 6 root causes, and a short list of enablers
answers them. An enabler is something that has to be true before AI capability can be
built on it safely and at scale.

[!box:The five enablers]

1. **Trusted information.** Connect the TMS read-only with scoped permissions, then
   decide the governed home for operating data with an owner and a date. Proved by
   the Monday margin pack generated rather than hand-built.
2. **Hosting and identity.** A service identity and an approved runtime, starting with
   the overnight rates pull that today runs on 1 analyst's laptop. Proved by that
   automation surviving its author's absence.
3. **Control and accountability.** A review-and-publish route and a shared
   verification pattern, so a working build becomes something the company can rely
   on. Proved by the booking-entry tool published on verified answers.
4. **Proof.** Collect the usage the current plan exposes, and baseline value measures
   before the first funded work starts. Proved by baselines on the quick win.
5. **Adoption and capacity.** A named accountable owner with protected time, and
   protected learning time for the 2-person IT team. Proved by teams actually testing
   what is built.

[!/box]

Two executive decisions come first. Decision A names one accountable owner with time
protected by a named executive. Decision B confirms the outcome the work serves,
growth-led or cost-led, because the 2 readings select different starting sets. Then
run the quick win, a bounded fix to the quote-to-booking handoff, which is open today
and carries the most quantified evidence in the discovery. Section 6 gives leadership
6 questions for choosing the delivery work that runs alongside, to be re-run whenever
circumstances change.

# 2. Why this work, and what it covered

Behind the engagement sits the commercial ambition. Whether Caldergate can win larger
importers depends in part on whether the business can move faster without creating
risk a retail client would refuse to carry.

AI creates value in a business in three ways, the three pillars used throughout this
report. Employees work smarter, processes run faster, and the product itself gets
better. This assessment was pointed at the first two; the service Caldergate sells was
left out of scope by agreement.

Caldergate enters with more in place than its leadership gives it credit for. An
interim AI usage note exists and contains a genuinely strong clause, competent review
before reliance. Adoption is bottom-up and real in every function visited. The
operations coordinator built a working booking-entry tool. The deputy finance lead
builds margin views with the assistant. The sales desk drafts quote emails with it
daily.

The work had three parts.

1. **The business discovery.** 7 conversations across the functions and 2 documents,
   recording every opportunity heard and mapping the work as value streams. A value
   stream is the path a piece of work travels through the business, from the event
   that triggers it to the outcome it exists to produce.
2. **The twenty-area readiness assessment.** It rated the organisation's groundwork
   area by area as in place, partial, gap or blocked.
3. **A hands-on working session.** The customs pre-check demonstration left the desk
   with a tested idea and this report with evidence of the condition for trusting it.

# 3. Where the value sits

The discovery answers one question. Where would the enabling layer pay back first?
The value concentrates in operations and the commercial desks. Most of it waits on
groundwork somewhere, so the groundwork has to be sequenced. And the first of that
groundwork is within reach now.

## 3.1 By function

Operations carries the most weighted opportunity mass, with IT next because so much
of the groundwork lands there, and the commercial functions close behind.

[!table-metrics]
| Function | Weighted opportunities |
|---|---|
| Operations | 5.0 |
| IT | 4.5 |
| Customer Service | 3.5 |
| Finance | 3.5 |
| Sales | 3.0 |
| Customs | 0.5 |

A few notes govern the table. Counts are share-weighted, so an opportunity split
across 2 functions counts half to each and nothing is double counted. Of the 21
opportunities, 7 carry a quantified figure, time spent or volume handled; the rest
are evidenced but not yet quantified. All quantified figures are self reported; no
timings were measured, which is itself a finding under Proof. The discovery records
no delivery status per opportunity, because there is no authoritative source of
delivery progress. The choosing session opens by asking the owning teams what is
genuinely moving. No cost implications have been assessed.

## 3.2 By value stream

Impact was graded at stream level against the first 2 pillars, how much friction,
manual effort and waiting a stream carries, and what removing it would release. 4
streams graded high and 2 medium. Effort was graded per opportunity as consultant
judgement; it indicates deliverability, and ranking by effort alone would surface the
easiest work rather than the most valuable.

An enabling gap is a piece of groundwork, such as a data connection or a review
process, that must be in place before the opportunities behind it can start. An
opportunity is gated when it waits on at least 1 enabling gap and open when it waits
on none; 13 of the 21 are gated and 8 are open. The stream tables count memberships:
19 memberships across the 18 opportunities that sit on a stream, because 1 sits on 2
streams, and 3 sit on none.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quote request to booked shipment | High | 5 | 4 | 3 |
| Booking to delivered consignment and clean invoice | High | 4 | 4 | 3 |

| Ad-hoc AI experiment to shared, trusted capability | Medium | 2 | 5 | 1 |

Every stream waits on at least 1 enabling gap somewhere along its path. That is
unusual, and it does not mean nothing can start: 8 opportunities are open, and they
cluster on the handoffs between teams, which is where the quick win below is built.
In flight and worth finishing rather than restarting: the CRM migration, half done,
which should carry part of the quote-to-booking handoff with it.

## 3.3 What the organisation can carry

Opportunities are only half the picture. The other half is whether the organisation
is ready to deliver them. The twenty-area readiness assessment groups its areas into
five themes, each rated in place, partial, gap or blocked. The five themes are the
five enablers above; the same frame runs from the assessment to the build plan.

|---|---|---|---|

| Hosting and identity | 0 | 1 | 3 |

No area is rated blocked, and none is yet rated in place, which is ordinary for a
business that adopted AI person by person. The partials are real positions, not

owner. Two facts stand out. The Proof theme is empty, all 4 areas rated gap, so

# 4. What binds the opportunities

Taken one at a time, the opportunities invite 21 separate arguments. Grouped by what
holds them back, they reduce to 6 root causes.

[!table-metrics]
| Root cause | How it shows up | Cleared by |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Data that cannot be reached | The margin pack hand-built in a day; dashboards fed by hand-exported CSVs; status answers gathered from 3 places | Trusted information |
| 2. One-off wins that cannot become shared, trusted capability | A working booking tool stranded on one machine; a FAQ assistant abandoned after 1 unverifiable answer | Control and accountability |
| 3. Work that stalls at handoffs | Quotes forwarded as whole email chains; escalations retyped at both ends; month-end chasing | Decisions and delivery choices rather than groundwork |
| 4. Unattended work that runs as a person | The rates pull on 1 analyst's laptop, which the official quoting process depends on | Hosting and identity |
| 5. Nothing yet provable | A console showing spend only; no usage, no baselines, no value measures | Proof |
| 6. Unowned capacity | A 2-person IT team carrying everything AI with no protected time, and no forum where these decisions get taken | Adoption and capacity |

Root causes of this kind outlive any organisational design, which is why the
enablers that clear them are the durable part of this report. The handoffs need
decisions rather than groundwork, and they hold the work that can start first.

# 5. The five enablers

Inside the five enablers, the discovery evidence sizes 6 enabling gaps, each with the
number of opportunities waiting on it. These are the discovery's sized gaps, rated
for the gap itself; they are not the twenty readiness areas, which is why the two
tables do not add up to each other. An opportunity waiting on more than 1 gap counts
against each, so the column can sum past the 13 gated opportunities.

[!table-metrics]
| Enabling gap | Enabler | Status | Opportunities waiting |
|---|---|---|---|
| A shared verification pattern so answers can be shown right before reliance | Control and accountability | Gap | 5 |
| A governed, queryable home for operating data | Trusted information | Gap | 4 |
| The TMS reachable read-only, permissions scoped per connection | Trusted information | Gap | 2 |
| A review-and-publish route from a staff-built tool to shared use | Control and accountability | Gap | 2 |
| Service identity and an approved runtime for unattended automations | Hosting and identity | Gap | 1 |
| Protected learning time for the IT team | Adoption and capacity | Gap | 0 |

**1. Trusted information.** The data Caldergate's teams need mostly sits where the
assistant cannot see it. The TMS holds the operational truth, the ledger the
financial truth, and nothing sits over both, in the finance lead's own words. The
consequences are the day-long Monday pack, about 6 hours a week of reconciliation,
and dashboards fed by hand-exported CSVs that are stale on creation. The first move
is the TMS connector, read-only and scoped, which the IT manager names as blocked on
permission thinking rather than technology. The second is a decision, with an owner
and a date, on a minimum useful home for operating data; 4 opportunities wait on it,
the most of any single gap bar verification. Proved by the margin pack generated.

**2. Hosting and identity.** Nothing unattended runs anywhere except 1 analyst's
laptop, under his personal logins. The official quoting process depends on it, and
the week he was off sick, sales quoted on a stale fuel surcharge. The moves are a
service identity and an approved managed runtime, with the rates pull as the first
candidate because it forces the identity question to be answered rather than
deferred. TMS permissions are coarse, everyone operational sees everything, so
scoping per connection comes before the first connector, while the estate is 1 item
long. Proved by the rates pull surviving its author's absence.

**3. Control and accountability.** People build things that work and nothing turns
them into capability the company can rely on. The booking-entry tool works on clean
emails and sits on 1 machine, stopped partly by permission uncertainty. The FAQ
assistant answered the most-asked question until 1 stale answer reached a customer,
and it died because nothing could show its answers were right before they went out.
The customs clerk and the sales lead both named verification as their condition for
trust, unprompted. The moves are a review-and-publish route, with the booking tool as
its first case, and 1 shared verification pattern, proved first on the cut-off
answers. The usage note's review clause already requires this; the work is
implementing a rule that exists. Proved by the booking tool published on verified
answers.

**4. Proof.** The admin console, reviewed together, shows spend for this month and
last and nothing else. No usage by person or team, no activity, no baselines, no
value measures. While cost is the only visible number, cost is the only conversation
available, which is precisely the sponsor's complaint. The moves are cheap: collect
what the current plan exposes before any tier conversation, derive cost attribution
by hand once at roughly 60 seats, and baseline 3 measures now, led by cycle time on
the quote-to-booking stream. Proved by baselines on the quick win.

**5. Adoption and capacity.** Adoption has been person by person and has gone a
long way on that basis. The ceiling is structural. IT is 2 people for 140 staff, the
manager describes himself as living in the backlog, and the builder of the best
prototype in the company stopped partly because he was unsure he was allowed. Name
one accountable owner for the enabler programme, protect their time, and protect
learning time for IT explicitly, because every other enabler lands on that team.
Proved by teams actually testing what is built.

# 6. Choosing where to start

This section is about what leadership decides first, the order the enablers build
in, and how to choose the delivery work that runs alongside. A route is a path
through a value stream; a portfolio is the set of routes funded together.

**The two decisions.** Decision A names one accountable owner for the enabler
programme, with time protected by a named executive who also protects IT's learning
time. It costs little and is the cheapest test of whether the organisation means to
move. Decision B confirms the outcome the work serves. A growth-led reading points
the first portfolio at the tender and quote streams, where the importers are won. A
cost-led reading points it at reporting and reconciliation. The 2 readings select
different sets from the same 21, so the choice comes before the set does.

**The three waves.** A wave is a group of moves that wait on nothing outside the
group. Wave one is open now: the verification pattern, the publish route, the
protected learning time, the AI surface inventory, the approval record, and the
measurement moves. Wave two is the permission and identity work: scoping, the TMS
connector, the service identity and runtime. Wave three is the operating-data home,
which starts once an owner with a mandate exists and the connector pattern is set.

[!box:Recommendation]

Never run an enabler bare. An enabler pays back only when a business outcome is
delivered across it, and the design details only surface under a real case. Pair
wave one with the quick win, wave two with the status-query and rates work it
unblocks, and wave three with the reporting stream it exists to serve.

[!/box]

**The quick win.** The quote-to-booking handoff is open today and carries the most
quantified evidence in the discovery. About 30 quotes a week are built in around 45
minutes each, roughly 20 bookings a day are entered at 10 to 15 minutes each, and
about a third arrive incomplete. A bounded fix, a completeness check at handoff and a
structured handoff replacing the forwarded chain, needs no enabling gap moved. The
booking-entry tool then follows through the wave-one publish route as its first
case. Measured against the team's own baseline, this is the proof the Proof enabler
needs.

## The choosing session

Six questions, asked in order, take the leadership team from 21 opportunities to a
defensible starting set in one working session, choosing among routes. The enablers
are not on the menu. They proceed in wave order regardless, and their gaps enter the
questions only as the gates that decide whether a route is open.

[!box:The six questions]

1. Where does unstarted value concentrate? Rank functions by weighted mass, then ask
   each owning team what is genuinely already moving.
2. Is the path open or gated? Open routes can start now, and gated routes are not
   lesser; their first step is the gate.
3. If gated, what does moving the gate release? Prefer gates that release many
   opportunities.
4. How strong is the evidence underneath? Deep evidence supports commitment; thin
   evidence supports a verification step before a build.
5. What does it cost to try? Grade effort route by route and use it to order the
   starting set. Effort decides the sequence and should never decide worth: a hard
   route at the top of the discovery still outranks an easy one at the bottom.
6. What does the route assume, and who owns testing it? State the assumption, name
   an owner, and test it before funding the work.

[!/box]

Before the session, the owning teams should re-confirm what is already moving, since
the evidence describes September 2027. The expected output is a portfolio of one
gate move, a few open-route starts, and one measurement move so results can be
proven. Re-run the session quarterly, at any change of structure or budget, and
whenever an enabling gap closes.

Six things this assessment advises against, whatever structure the business takes:

1. Do not build a weighted composite score to rank the opportunities. The weights
   would be invented rather than evidenced, and the argument shifts from the work
   itself to the scoring formula.
2. Do not plan or grade effort for a whole stream at once. Commit route by route.
3. Do not ask this evidence to justify role-level decisions. It was gathered under a
   productivity scope and describes friction in processes; it says nothing about
   whether any role is needed.
4. Do not add new investment where work is already moving. The CRM migration should
   carry the handoff work it already covers.
5. Do not wait for the operating-data question to resolve before starting. 8
   opportunities are open, and once the two decisions are made, wave one needs
   nothing more than the will to run it.
6. Do not put assistant output into customer-facing paths before the verification
   pattern and audit evidence exist. The FAQ assistant already demonstrated why, at
   the cost of one missed sailing.

# Annex B. Review gate rules and the verification pattern

**The review gate.** The observation here was a working booking-entry tool stranded
on one machine, its builder unsure he was allowed, and a first publication route
nowhere in sight. The recommendation is a review gate before company-wide
publication, owned by a named person, built as a one-page rulebook for authors and a
checklist for reviewers. The rules that matter most:

- A tool never touches credentials and never fetches instructions at run time.
- It holds only the narrowest permissions it needs, and treats everything it reads
  as data rather than instruction.
- It stops for confirmation before irreversible actions, and describes itself
  accurately.
- A change reaches users only through a version increase, so every published
  capability stays traceable to what was reviewed.
- A good tool records what a new colleague could not discover, the organisation's
  own rules and boundaries, rather than technique the tools already carry.

**The verification pattern.** The observation was an assistant giving a confidently
wrong answer with nothing to catch it before a customer did. The recommendation is
one shared pattern, a harness that runs cases, applies checks and reports results,
with each tool supplying only its own cases and expected answers, agreed by the
people who know what correct looks like. Deterministic checks come first, and
twenty to fifty realistic cases per tool is a practical start. Sign-off records its
own boundary: what passes is a specific capability against specific criteria, never
AI for a function in general.

**Together.** The gate, the pattern and the Proof measures are three controls, all
within reach now. The practical order is the gate first, the pattern alongside it,
and the measures switched on before the first published capability is used in anger.

# Annex C. Identity and hosting

Identity for AI has three layers, and they fail independently. Layer one is who can
sign in, which works today through the office suite's sign-in. Layer two is what a
signed-in person's assistant can reach. Connectors inherit each person's
permissions, so the assistant would be exactly as trustworthy as the TMS permission
model, which is currently coarse; scoping per connection comes first. Layer three is
what runs when nobody is signed in. Agents need identities of their own, and
Caldergate's one unattended automation runs as its author, which is secure by
default and the scaling limit. Own as little of the machinery as the data allows:
start on a managed runtime with the rates pull, and revisit self-hosting only if a
case proves unsuitable, with the date agreed now.

# Annex D. Value stream mapping method

The 2 step-level maps that accompany this report walk one real, recent case each,
with the trigger on the left, the outcome on the right, and the steps as the work
actually happens. On every step they carry who does it, the working time where
stated, the pain on a simple scale, and the system it lives in. Nothing has been
estimated to fill a gap; a step with no figures had none in the evidence. Read a map
by its waits and its pain rather than its busiest steps. The streams were derived
from conversations rather than drawn in a room with the teams, so the important ones
deserve redrawing with the teams, adding the timing, before committing.
