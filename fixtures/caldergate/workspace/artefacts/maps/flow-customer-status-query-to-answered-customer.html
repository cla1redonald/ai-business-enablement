Customer status query to answered customer

From a customer's where-is-it query to an answered customer and any escalation resolved, in 6 steps: how this work travels today, and where it hurts.

6 steps  ·  pain scored on 0  ·  timing on 1  ·  constraint at step 2. Full reading notes at the foot of the page.
Why that step is the constraint

Step 2, gathering the answer across three systems, is the constraint.

  • The evidence: the only timed step, paid on every one of about 60 queries a day: about 5 minutes clean and 20 to 30 with a problem, all self reported.
  • Corroboration: the most-asked question (sailing cut-offs) lives in a spreadsheet that is sometimes updated late, and the one attempt to automate answers died on exactly that staleness.
  • The effect: the customer is on hold while the agent swivels, so the step's cost is paid in customer time as well as desk time, on every query.
  • What would strengthen the call: a measured split of clean versus problem queries. The 60-a-day volume and both timings are self reported, so the call rests on consistent tellings rather than measurement.
  • Also unmeasured: no waits were recorded anywhere on this stream, including the escalation cycle in steps 4 to 6, which nobody can currently track at all. That absence is itself a finding.

A FAQ assistant was tried and abandoned after one confidently stale cut-off answer reached a customer; the team's stated condition for trying again is answers that can be shown right reliably.

  • Cut-off answers, verified and current (gated on the verification pattern).
