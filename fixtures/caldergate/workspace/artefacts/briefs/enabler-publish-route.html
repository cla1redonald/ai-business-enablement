A review-and-publish route from a staff-built tool to shared use

People are already building things that work; what is missing is any route from one person's machine to something the company can rely on, so the best builds strand where they were made.

Enabler: Control and accountability · Status: Gap · Opportunities waiting: 2 · Owner: to be named · Wave: 1

Skills this needs

Skills are derivable from the work. Days are not, without knowing scope and who is doing it, so no estimate is given: putting a number against work nobody has been asked to do would imply a decision that has not been made.

The review judgement already exists: the IT manager's approval decisions are sane and trusted, which is why the estate has stayed clean at low volume. What does not exist is an hour a week in which to run a route, which is the capacity gap wearing different clothes.

What is missing, and why

The strongest prototype in the company, a booking-entry tool that extracts TMS fields from forwarded email chains, works on clean emails and sits on one machine. Its builder stopped developing it for two stated reasons: nowhere to put it where others could use it, and uncertainty about whether he was allowed.

The same absence killed the FAQ assistant by a different route: it went from working to abandoned with no review step in between that might have caught the staleness risk before a customer did.

A route with review in front of it is what turns one person's win into company capability, and it raises the return on every other item in this report: whatever gets built next for quoting, status answers or document checks reaches everyone through it, or reaches nobody.

Routes

Options, not a recommendation. Each states what it assumes, because the assumption is usually the thing to test first.

A shared folder, a one-page rulebook and a review checklist

impact: higheffort: low

The smallest real route: published tools live in one shared place, a one-page rulebook tells authors what publishable means, and the IT manager's existing judgement is written down as the review checklist, with evaluation evidence required at submission.

What it assumes. One protected hour a week for review. That is the protected-time gap, and this route makes the cost of not granting it concrete.

Versioned publication with a named owner per tool

impact: mediumeffort: medium

The fuller shape: a change reaches users only through a version increase, every published tool has a named owner, and the record of what was reviewed protects the business later. The natural second step once the simple route has volume.

What it assumes. Enough publication volume to justify the ceremony. Premature at 1 tool; right at 10.
