# Call summary: customs working session

**Date:** 2027-09-29 · **Sub-type:** consultant-led session · **Present:** [CONSULTANT-1], [CUSTOMS-CLERK-1], [OPS-LEAD-1]

## What the session covered

One real declaration walked end to end, the bounce loop, and a live demonstration of
mechanical document checks. Consultant-led: the demo is the consultant's work; the
clerk's statements are the evidence.

## Processes described

- **Declarations.** About 40 a week, 25 minutes for a straightforward one, typed
  into the customs platform from the customer's invoice and packing list (self
  reported).
- **The bounce loop.** About 1 in 5 bounces on customer paperwork mismatches:
  another 15 minutes of rework plus a wait of up to a day, usually while the
  consignment is already rolling, at which point it is an emergency.

## Numbers said (verbatim, by role)

- [CUSTOMS-CLERK-1]: "Twenty-five minutes for a straightforward one, and we do
  about 40 a week."
- [CUSTOMS-CLERK-1]: "About 1 in 5 declarations bounces because something on the
  customer's invoice doesn't add up."

## The mechanical-checks point

The failing checks are mechanical (totals match, origin stated, description
recognisable) and could run when documents land at the booking desk: "If the booking
desk had a checklist run against the documents when they land, I'd get clean files."

## The demo, and the condition

A live run on fictionalised documents caught a weight mismatch and a vague
description. The clerk's condition for trust: right every time, because "a wrong
all-clear is worse than no check."
