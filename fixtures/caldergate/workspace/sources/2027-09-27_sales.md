S1: Walk me through a quote, end to end.

S2: A request lands, email nine times out of ten. Someone on the desk opens the rate cards, which are spreadsheets on Filestead, one per trade lane, finds the applicable rates, checks the surcharges are current, builds the quote in our template and sends it. Honest answer, 45 minutes for a normal one. We do about 30 a week.

S1: Elena reckoned about 20 minutes.

S2: Elena's remembering the easy ones. A repeat customer on a lane we price every week, sure, 20. A new lane means hunting for rates, and anything with customs complications means asking Femi, and now you're at an hour plus. The written process note says 30 minutes and I'd love to meet the person who wrote it.

S1: What happens when a quote is accepted?

S2: I forward the thread to operations. The whole chain, because the details are scattered through it: the original request, the revisions, the final agreed rate. Aileen's team pull it apart at their end. I know that's ugly. Tallybook is supposed to fix some of this, the pipeline's half in there now, but bookings still start life as a forwarded email.

S1: RFQs from the bigger prospects?

S2: The painful growth work. A retail importer sends a tender spreadsheet, two hundred lanes, fill in your rates. That's two people for most of a week, copying between their format and ours. We win maybe one in four, and I sometimes wonder if we'd win more if the people doing the copying had the week back to actually think about pricing.

S1: What have you tried with AI?

S2: Drafting quote emails, and it's good at that. Anything that touches the rates I've kept it away from, deliberately, because if it invents a number we eat the difference. If it could read the actual rate cards and only the actual rate cards, that changes things.
