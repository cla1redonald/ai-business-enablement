S1: Which two things eat the most time?

S2: The Monday margin pack, and reconciliation. The margin pack is a full day, every week, most of it mine or Maya's. We export completed jobs out of Loadstone, export the ledger from Ledgerline, and marry them up in a spreadsheet. The marrying up is the day: job references don't match invoice references cleanly, so it's lookup after lookup and a lot of eyeballing.

S1: And it goes to Elena on Monday?

S2: Monday morning, and about one week in four something's wrong in it. Usually a job that invoiced after the export was taken, or a rate that got queried and changed in one system but not the other. We find out when someone challenges a number.

S3: Reconciliation generally is about 6 hours a week on top of that. Same disease, different limbs: the same shipment exists in Loadstone, in Ledgerline, and in whatever spreadsheet the desk kept, and they drift.

S1: Where does the data you'd want actually live?

S2: There's no one place, that's the honest answer. Loadstone knows the operational truth, Ledgerline knows the financial truth, and nothing sits over both. We're careful with customers' shipment data, that's drilled into everyone. Our own numbers get a lot less ceremony.

S1: Maya, Elena mentioned you'd been building something.

S3: I got fed up rebuilding the same charts, so I've been making Verrin build the margin views for me. It works, but it's working off a CSV I export by hand, so it's stale the moment I make it. What I actually want is a live feed it could read, the cut-off dates and the rates and the job statuses. If those were reachable, half the Monday pack builds itself.

S2: For the record, I'd want some controls before anything reads the ledger directly.

S1: What happens at month end?

S2: Chasing. Two or three days of asking desks to confirm jobs are really closed so we can invoice. It's not analysis, it's shepherding, and it's the same every month.
