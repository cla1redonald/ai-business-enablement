S1: Femi, show me one declaration, start to finish, on a real recent one.

S2: This one from Tuesday. The booking's in Loadstone, the commercial invoice and packing list came in on the email, and Portwick is where the declaration gets made. I'm reading off the invoice and typing into Portwick: commodity codes, values, weights, origin. Twenty-five minutes for a straightforward one, and we do about 40 a week.

S1: What goes wrong?

S2: The paperwork. About 1 in 5 declarations bounces because something on the customer's invoice doesn't add up: weights that don't match the packing list, a missing origin statement, a commodity description too vague to code. Then I'm emailing the customer, waiting, and redoing it. Call it another fifteen minutes of my time per bounce, plus the wait, which can be a day.

S1: Could the checking happen before it reaches you?

S2: That's the dream. The checks are mechanical, does the total match, is the origin stated, is the description one of the ones we've seen before. If the booking desk had a checklist run against the documents when they land, I'd get clean files.

S3: Worth saying, when a declaration bounces, the consignment is usually already rolling. That's when it becomes an emergency and my phone rings.

S1: Let's try something while we're here. I'll have Verrin read this invoice and packing list, both fictionalised, and check the three things you named. [works] There. It caught the weight mismatch and flagged the description.

S2: That's the checking part of my morning, yes. I'd want it to be right every time before I trusted it, mind. A wrong all-clear is worse than no check.

S1: Agreed, and that's a solvable problem, it's called verification and it's exactly what I'd want wrapped around this before anyone relies on it. Good session, thank you both.
