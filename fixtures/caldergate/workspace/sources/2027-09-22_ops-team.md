S1: Talk me through a normal day. Not the org chart version, the actual version.

S2: Bookings, mostly. Confirmed quotes come across from sales, nearly always as a forwarded email chain, and we get them into Loadstone. On a normal day that's about 20 bookings across the desk.

S1: How long does one take?

S3: Ten to fifteen minutes if the email has everything. I'm copying each line across by hand, collection address, delivery window, pallet count, the agreed rate. If something's missing I'm back into the thread digging, or on the phone to Morag's lot, and then it's half an hour easily.

S1: What do you do when it arrives wrong or incomplete?

S3: Chase. Maybe a third arrive missing something. The rate's the worst one, if the rate in the email doesn't match the rate card we have to stop and query it, because once it's in Loadstone the invoice picks it up.

S2: The other half of the day is waiting on carriers. We put a booking request out and confirmation can take four hours to come back. Nothing moves in that window, and the customer is usually chasing us before the carrier has answered.

S1: When Sam showed me earlier, he was copying each booking line by line out of an email into Loadstone with both windows side by side. Is that the standard way, or was that a bad one?

S3: No, that's the job. That's what booking entry is.

S1: It sounds like the rekeying is your real bottleneck, more than the carrier waits.

S2: Maybe. Honestly the waits hurt more on the bad days, but the keying is every day.

S1: What happens when something needs escalating?

S2: Phone. If a consignment's in trouble, whoever spots it rings me, and I ring the carrier, and then I'm typing a note into Loadstone after the fact so there's a record. Tessa's team will tell you the same from their side, they hear about problems from the customer before we've told them.

S1: Anything you've tried with AI?

S2: Sam had a go at getting Verrin to read a booking email and spit out the Loadstone fields.

S3: It worked on clean emails. But I didn't have anywhere to put it where the others could use it, so it's a thing on my machine. And I wasn't sure I was allowed, to be honest, so I stopped.

S1: Thanks, Aileen. Sam, that email-to-fields thing, send me what you built if you still have it.
